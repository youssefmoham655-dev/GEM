Famous Pharaohs of Egypt

Tutankhamun

Reigned c. 1332–1323 BC: Died around age 18 as the third-to-last pharaoh of the 18th Dynasty.

Reversed Reforms: Restored traditional religion, moved the capital back to Memphis, and changed his name from Tutankhaten.

Tomb KV62: Buried in a small, non-royal tomb due to his sudden death from malaria and a leg fracture.

1922 Discovery: Discovered by Howard Carter and Lord Carnarvon with over 5,000 intact artifacts.

Grand Egyptian Museum: His golden mask and treasures were relocated there by 2025.

Ramses II

Reign: Ruled for 66 years (1279–1213 BC) as the third pharaoh of the 19th Dynasty.

Achievements: Widely celebrated as Ramesses the Great, a successful warrior king, and builder of major monuments like Abu Simbel.

Death & Mummy: Died around age 90–91 and is now housed at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo.

Hatshepsut

Reign (1479–1457 BC): Sixth pharaoh of the 18th Dynasty and Egypt's second confirmed female ruler in her own right.

Rise to Power: Started as regent for her stepson Thutmose III before assuming the full position of pharaoh and adopting male attributes.

Achievements: Oversaw a peaceful and prosperous era, constructing major monuments including her famous mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari.

Legacy: Died around Year 22 of her reign, after which later rulers attempted to erase her from official records.

Cleopatra VII

Reign (51–30 BC): Queen of Egypt and the last active Hellenistic pharaoh of the Ptolemaic dynasty.

Alliances & Relationships: Maintained political and romantic alliances with Julius Caesar (producing a son, Caesarion) and later Mark Antony (producing three children).

Downfall & Death: After Octavian declared war and defeated their fleet at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC, Antony and Cleopatra were defeated in Egypt in 30 BC, leading Antony to commit suicide and Cleopatra to follow shortly after, likely by poisoning.
